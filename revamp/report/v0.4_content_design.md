# v0.4 Content Design — The Learning Progression

**Version:** 0.4 · **Status:** research-derived learning journey (prototype, not production)
**Principle (from the blueprint):** difficulty rises via *rule complexity, constraints, and near-miss distractors* — never via bigger numbers, harder words, or longer wording.

---

## 1. From "six buckets of puzzles" to a path

v0.3 was a correct **cross-section**: every domain and level appeared, but items were not ordered for a learner.
v0.4 reorganises all 50 items into **29 Skill-Practice progressions**. Each progression is one skill that develops:

```
Explore  ->  Think  ->  Challenge  ->  Master
(accessible)  (multi-step)  (complex)     (non-routine, integrates strategies)
```

The flagship example is **Working Backwards (6.1)**, now a full 4-step path:

| Step | Item | What the child does |
|---|---|---|
| Explore | 0044 | Reverse **one** operation (12 − 5). |
| Think | 0028 | Reverse **two** operations in order (÷2 then −3). |
| Challenge | 0045 | Reverse two changes inside a **story** (bus stops). |
| Master | 0046 | Reverse a 2-stage process **and verify** forward (combines WB + check). |

This is the product shape the reviewer asked for: **skill → practise → feedback → harder variation → mastery → transfer** — something a parent reads as *learning*, not entertainment.

Other progressions with 3+ steps: 1.1 (Explore/Think/Challenge), 1.2, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 4.1, 5.1, 5.2, 5.4, 6.2, 6.6. Single-item progressions (e.g. 2.5, 3.2, 4.2, 4.4, 4.6, 4.7, 5.3, 5.5, 6.3) are intentional: those skills are Tier-2/anchor points and get deeper coverage in the scaled 100+ build.

## 2. Why each level exists (and why a question sits there)
Every item carries `thinking_skill` + `why_level` so the intention is never implicit. Examples:
- **Explore** items are *scaffolds*, not "easy wins": 0032 (+3 analogy) teaches the analogy format before compound rules; 0039 rotates an asymmetric L so orientation matters.
- **Think** items add a second rule or constraint: 0025 maps two features; 0007 merges partial orders.
- **Challenge** items change context or add a dimension: 0045 puts WB in a story; 0043 runs two matrix rules at once.
- **Master** items (0046, 0050) are where the familiar algorithm fails and the child must **combine** moves — the transfer ceiling.

## 3. Coverage vs the framework
- **Tier-1 fully represented:** 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.1, 3.3, 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.6, 5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 6.1, 6.2, **6.6** (the last was the audit's MISSING gap — now filled).
- **Research-flagged visuals present:** 3×3 matrix (0043), 3D orientation (0021), combining/hidden (0027).
- **Singapore heuristics present:** working-backwards, guess-&-check, before-after, pattern-application.
- **Master tier present** (was MISSING): 0046, 0050.
- Verbal deliberately capped (6 items) per the blueprint — we do not become an English workbook.

## 4. How a session would actually feel (product shape)
- **Daily Brain Challenge:** auto-pull ~5 items across progressions, mostly Explore–Challenge, so a child meets many skills weekly.
- **Skill Practice:** e.g. tap "Working Backwards" -> get 0044 → 0028 → 0045 → 0046 in order, with feedback after each.
- **Progress:** a per-domain strength radar; a badge when a progression reaches Master.
- None of this is app code; it is the *content contract* the renderer and data model must serve.

## 5. The 12-week question (required answer)

> **If a P3 high-ability child uses BrainActive for 12 weeks, what specific thinking skills should improve, and how does our content cause that improvement?**

**What improves (concrete, observable skills):**

1. **Pattern sensitivity.** The child stops guessing and starts *looking at the structure* — differences between terms (1.1), interleaved rules, cycles with a second attribute (5.1), and repeating rules applied to far positions (6.6). Caused by: repeated Explore→Think practice where the reward is *finding the rule*, not computing.
2. **Named heuristics they can reach for.** After 12 weeks the child owns a small toolkit: *work backwards* (6.1), *guess & check* (6.2), *before-after / constant difference* (6.3), *apply the pattern* (6.6). Caused by: each heuristic is its own progression with a Master step that forces transfer to an unfamiliar context (e.g. 0045 bus story), so the heuristic becomes a habit, not a one-off trick.
3. **Spatial fluency.** Mental rotation of asymmetric shapes (4.1), reflection (4.2), folding nets (4.3), 3D orientation (4.4), and assembling/composing figures (4.7). Caused by: visual items are deterministic and repeatable; the child practises the *mental move* until it is automatic — a skill school barely trains.
4. **Logical discipline.** Contrapositive and chained conditionals (2.3), merging partial orders (2.1), constraint matching (2.2), must-be-true from combined conditions (3.5). Caused by: distractors are near-miss traps, so the child learns to *check the inference*, not the first plausible answer.
5. **Figural reasoning.** Matrices with row+column rules (5.2), SPONCS classification (5.3), dual-feature analogy (5.4). Caused by: the items escalate from single-feature to dual-feature to 3×3, training the eye to hold two dimensions.

**How the content causes it (the mechanism):** the improvement is *not* from 50 clever questions. It comes from the **ordered path** — each skill is met first as an accessible move (Explore), then stressed with a second rule or constraint (Think), then placed in a novel context (Challenge), then forced to combine with another strategy (Master). Feedback after each item closes the loop. Over 12 weeks of short daily sessions, the child accumulates **transferable strategies** and the *habit* of asking "which heuristic?" before "what formula?" — which is exactly what a strong P3 child needs and school does not systematically teach.

That is the product identity: **Singapore P3 high-ability thinking practice as a journey**, not generic brain games.
