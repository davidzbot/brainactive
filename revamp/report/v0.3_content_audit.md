# v0.3 Content Audit — Research-Derived Curriculum Review

**Date:** 2026-08-27 · **Status:** v0.3 kept as *research prototype* (do **not** build DB / renderer / 100 from it yet)
**Method:** every item re-classified against `brainactive_p3_skill_framework.md` (Tiers 1–3) and
`brainactive_p3_content_blueprint.md` (level model + first-100 weighting). Judgment criterion is the
product promise, not "is it a valid puzzle": *does this item, at its level, make a P3 high-ability child
think in a way school does not?*

## Verdict up front
- v0.3 is **research-mapped** (every item carries a real `skill_code` and the domain mix tracks the blueprint)
  but **not research-designed**: 22 of 30 are carried over v0.2 items, re-selected to fit the map. That is
  acceptable for a prototype and was stated honestly in the self-review. It is **not** a finished curriculum.
- **8 items are REWRITE** (valid but below the HA bar — including all 7 the reviewer flagged). **0 are REPLACE.**
- **MISSING:** Tier-1 skill `6.6 Pattern Application` is absent; only one (2×2) matrix where research flags 3×3
  as important; **no Master-tier item at all** (blueprint reserves ~8/100 for non-routine); no 4.5 Position,
  6.4 Draw-Diagram, 6.5 Make-List.
- The bigger gap is structural: v0.3 is a **flat cross-section**, not a **learning journey** (see §4).

## 2. The 8 REWRITE items — concrete redesign specs

The shared principle (from the blueprint): *difficulty rises via rule complexity, constraints, and near-miss
distractors — not bigger numbers or harder words.* Each rewrite below adds exactly that.

**0003 (1.2 Numerical Relationships) — was ×4 analogy.**
Rewrite: "2 is to 5 as 4 is to ___?" where the rule is `×2 + 1` (2→5, so 4→9), distractors 8 (×2), 10 (+? off-by-one), 11 (×2+3). Now the child must **infer a two-part rule**, not spot a round multiplier. Keeps skill 1.2, raises Think→ appropriately HA.

**0007 (2.1 Deductive Ordering) — was a read-off chain.**
Rewrite: give two partial orders that must be **merged**: "A is faster than B. C is slower than D. B is faster than C. E is between A and B." Child constructs A > E > B > C > D. Adds one transitive inference step; still Explore/Think, but no longer mechanical.

**0010 (2.4 Classification) — was curved-vs-straight.**
Rewrite: classify by **two dimensions at once** — e.g., four figures where three are "4-sided and red" and one is "4-sided and blue" (or "3-sided and red"); the odd-one-out requires attending to *both* shape-count and colour. Lowers the ceiling-busting obviousness while staying Explore.

**0013 (3.1 Word Analogy) — was bird:wing::fish:fin.**
Rewrite: a less cliché relation, e.g. **function/instrument**: "lock is to key as __ is to __" (answer: door is to handle? better: "pen is to write as __ is to cut" → knife). Or a **cause→effect / part→whole** that isn't in every IQ book. Goal: the relation isn't guessable from the first pair's surface familarity.

**0016 (3.5 Sentence Logic) — was trivial modus ponens, mis-levelled Challenge.**
Rewrite (and re-level to Think): "Mei reads every Saturday. She also reads whenever it rains. Today she is NOT reading. What MUST be true?" → It is not Saturday AND not raining (must-be-true from negation of an OR). Requires applying the contrapositive across two conditions; that is the HA move. Drop to Think.

**0017 (4.1 2D Rotation) — was corner-dot 90°.**
Rewrite: use an **asymmetric shape** (e.g., an L or arrow) and ask after **two successive 90° turns**, OR rotate then ask which of four oriented options matches — so orientation (not just a corner label) must be tracked. Still Explore but demands actual mental rotation.

**0022 (5.1 Figure Sequence) — was a cycle that returns to "up".**
Rewrite: predict a **later step** (e.g., "after 7 clockwise turns, where does the arrow point?") and add a **second changing attribute** (colour alternates each turn). Now it's prediction + dual tracking, not reciting a cycle. Keeps 5.1, raises to Think.

**0025 (5.4 Figure Analogy) — was single-feature (size) mapping.**
Rewrite: vary **two features** — "small red square → large blue square; so small green circle → ?" (large blue circle: size flips AND colour flips, shape preserved). Demands mapping two transformations; that is the real figure-analogy skill. Keeps 5.4, raises to Think.

## 4. The product question — "what does the child learn in 3 months?"

> *If a parent pays for BrainActive for a strong P3 child, what will the child learn to think better after 3 months?*

A high-ability P3 child already reasons; what they lack is **named, systematic, transferable strategies** and the
**habit** of reaching for them. After 3 months of the *intended* product (Daily Brain Challenge + Skill Practice +
level progression — not v0.3's flat sample), the child should be able to:

1. **Articulate patterns** (numerical, figural) instead of guessing — pattern sensitivity as a deliberate act.
2. **Name and apply heuristics** to non-routine problems: work backwards, guess-&-check, before-after, draw-a-model,
   make-a-list. This is the Singapore "thinking skills" vocabulary that school touches but rarely drills.
3. **Mentally manipulate space** — rotate, reflect, fold/unfold, assemble — with confidence (spatial fluency school
   barely trains, and a genuine differentiator vs generic apps).
4. **Follow and construct logic** — if-then, contrapositive, must-be-true, constraint-matching — reasoning discipline.
5. **Transfer / meta-skill** — meet a word problem and ask *"which heuristic?"* before *"what formula?"*

**The critical caveat:** v0.3 does **not** yet deliver this journey. It is a correct *cross-section* (every level and
domain appears) but it is **not sequenced per child**: there is no Skill-Practice scaffolding (5 items L1→L3 within one
subskill), no spaced progression, no Daily-Brain-Challenge pull logic, and no Master ceiling to aim at. The six domains
are the right *buckets*; the product value is the *ordered path through them*. Until the content is redesigned as a
path (v0.4: REWRITE the 8, ADD the 3 missing, and group into Skill-Practice progressions), we are selling six buckets
of puzzles, not a learning journey.
