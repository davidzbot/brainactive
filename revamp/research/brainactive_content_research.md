## 1. The Singapore context

### GEP / High-Ability identification
- The Gifted Education Programme (GEP) historically identified P3 pupils in **two rounds**:
  - **Screening** (all P3 pupils): English + Mathematics.
  - **Selection** (shortlisted): English + Mathematics + **General Ability** (GA).
- From **2026**, MOE moves to a **single-stage national identification exercise** for P3
  pupils that emphasises *reasoning ability, language proficiency, and problem-solving
  potential* rather than pure syllabus mastery (Terry Chew Academy, 2026).
- Separately, schools run **High Ability Learners (HAL)** enrichment (e.g. E2K Math/Science,
  Maths Expert @ Work). HAL and GEP both reward *how a child thinks*, not memorised types.

**Product implication:** Position BrainActive as **"high-ability-style reasoning practice"**,
not "GEP test prep." This is accurate, broader (covers HAL too), and avoids official-claim risk.

### General Ability paper (the core skill set)
Public enrichment sources consistently describe GA as ~30–40 items in ~75 minutes, split into:
- **GA (English / Verbal):** word relations & analogies, anagrams, number–letter decoding,
  verbal logic/deduction.
- **GA (Math / Non-verbal):** patterns & sequences, odd-one-out / sets, spatial awareness
  (rotation, reflection, 3D nets), logical reasoning.

Sources stress that GA is **IQ-like**: it rewards familiarity with *unfamiliar* formats and
pattern vocabulary, not syllabus drilling.

## 2. Question-type families observed

| Family | Types seen in sources | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal reasoning | analogies, anagrams, decoding/cipher, sentence logic, odd-word-out, vocabulary manipulation | GA (English) |
| Numerical reasoning | number patterns/sequences, number analogies, missing-number relationships, cryptarithms, comparative/deductive quantity problems | GA (Math) + Screening Math |
| Non-verbal / visual | pattern completion, rotation, reflection, 3D nets, odd-one-out (shape), shape analogies | GA (Math) |
| Logical reasoning | ordering, classification, conditional deduction, constraint/matching, syllogisms | across both |
| Spatial | mentally rotate 2D/3D, reflect, fold nets | GA (Math) |

**SPONCS** (Shape, Position, Orientation, Number, Colour, Size) is the widely taught heuristic
for analysing visual pattern items — useful as a generator dimension later.

## 3. Difficulty & what makes items suitable for high-ability P3

- Items are **non-routine**: they do not resemble textbook examples; the bottleneck is
  reasoning, not computation (children at P3 already have enough arithmetic fluency).
- Strong items have a **single clean rule**, plausible distractors that reflect common
  mis-steps (e.g. spotting the wrong sub-pattern), and a short, explainable solution path.
- Avoid: long reading passages, obscure cultural references, trivia, and "trick" wording.
- The 2026 shift means **reasoning fluency** (not past-paper drilling) is the differentiator —
  which aligns perfectly with BrainActive's "thinking skill development" principle.

## 5. Guardrails for our content
- Build **original** items inspired by these skills; never replicate a commercial question's
  wording, choices, diagram, or distinctive construction.
- Use **structured `visual_spec`** (grid / shape / transformation) so diagrams can be rendered
  deterministically as SVG/Canvas later — no raster images at this stage.
- Label difficulty for a *high-ability P3* audience: `easy` = accessible-for-strong-P3, not
  ordinary homework; most items `medium`/`hard`; a few `challenge`.
