## 2. SOURCE EVIDENCE — observed Singapore GEP/GA verbal archetypes

**E1. Verbal analogies (category, function, part-whole, number-shape).**
- S3: *Cannon, rifle, pistol : river, stream, ___* → answer **ocean** (set/domain analogy: weapons ↔ bodies of water).
- S7 / Joyous tip: *FOUR is to SQUARE as THREE is to ___* → **TRIANGLE** (number-of-sides analogy; but note the word appears *in isolation* — vocabulary-dependent).
- S5/S6 list "Word Relation / analogies" as a core GA (English) type.
- S9/S10 (CogAT) list Verbal Analogies as one of three verbal subtests.

**E2. Word transformation / anagrams / hidden words.**
- S1: "form words using letters which may be scrambled up, hidden in other words, or through transformation."
- S5/S8: Anagram explicitly named as a GA type; "scrambling and unscrambling letters … indicates ability to think flexibly."

**E3. Codes & ciphers (letter/number decoding).**
- S4: letters↔numbers with a hidden rule (right = square of left; Y=25).
- S1: "cipher … given one or more examples of words written in a code, encipher or decipher another."
- S5: "Decoding: finding the relationship between numbers and letters."

**E4. Verbal deduction / logic.**
- S1: comprehension/inference where "logical understanding of the points raised" is tested.
- S5/S6: "Logic: deducing a conclusion from information provided."
- S8: verbal form of logical reasoning ("read a few statements and draw a conclusion").

**E5. Sentence completion.**
- S6: "Sentence completion: choose the right word to fill a sentence based on context and grammar."
- S9/S10 (CogAT): Sentence Completion subtest.

**E6. Vocabulary (isolated).**
- S7: an entire *vocabulary* section in the English component; analogy words can appear in isolation (triangle example) so "you need a wide vocabulary."

## 3. ANALYSIS — vocabulary vs verbal reasoning (A / B / C)

| Archetype | Class | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Synonym/antonym drill, "what does X mean?" | **A — Pure vocabulary** | Recalls word meaning; low reasoning. **Not a BrainActive priority.** |
| Analogy where the relationship is clear but the words are rare (FOUR/SQUARE/TRIANGLE) | **B — Vocabulary + reasoning** | Relationship is reasoning; but a missing word blocks it. Valuable *if* words stay P3-accessible. |
| Category/function analogy with familiar words (Cannon→ocean) | **C — Genuine verbal reasoning** | Child discovers the relation; vocabulary is everyday. **High priority.** |
| Letter/number code, cipher, anagram, hidden word, letter series | **C — Genuine verbal reasoning** | Operates on *letters/structure*, not word meaning. Low vocabulary burden. **High priority; differentiates us from English tuition.** |
| Verbal deduction from 2–3 clues | **C — Genuine verbal reasoning** | Pure inference; can use simple words. **High priority.** |
| Sentence completion (cloze) | **B — Vocabulary + reasoning** | Needs context + grammar; leans on vocabulary. Use *lightly*, capped. |

**ANALYSIS takeaway:** BrainActive should weight **C** heavily and use **B** sparingly with P3-accessible words. **A is excluded.** This is consistent with the product identity ("reasoning through language", not "knowledge of English").

## 4. SOURCE EVIDENCE — international comparison (CogAT, UK GL)

- **CogAT (S9/S10):** Verbal Battery = Analogies + Sentence Completion + Classification. Design goal: *"reasoning skills students have developed to solve problems they have not been directly taught."* Supports our emphasis on relationship discovery over knowledge.
- **UK GL 21 types (S11/S12/S13):** rich menu — word/letter codes, hidden words, letter series, move-a-letter, anagrams, odd-one-out, closest/opposite meaning. Many are **low-vocabulary, high-structure** (codes, hidden words, letter series) — exactly the kind BrainActive can generate deterministically.

**ANALYSIS — what Singapore material under-represents (useful additions):**
Singapore GA prep sources emphasise analogy + anagram + decoding + logic, but give **less systematic treatment** of (a) letter-series/alphabet patterns, (b) hidden-word/move-a-letter manipulations, (c) standalone odd-one-out training. These are well-developed in UK GL and map cleanly to a deterministic text generator. **We adopt them as supplementary C-type skills** — but Singapore evidence remains primary; we do not let UK material redefine the product.

---

## 5. BRAINACTIVE RECOMMENDATION (summary; detail in the framework doc)

- Treat Verbal Reasoning as a **core domain** with ~7 skills, dominated by **C (genuine reasoning)** and light **B**.
- Lead with: **Analories, Classification, Codes & Ciphers, Word Manipulation (anagrams/hidden), Verbal Deduction** — all low-vocabulary, high-reasoning, and generatable.
- Cap **Sentence Completion / vocabulary-dependent** items; never become a synonym drill.
- Keep verbal **distinct from Math Hero** (Math Hero owns maths drilling); verbal here is *language-as-structure*, not comprehension.
- Allocation within first 100: recommend **~15%** (see allocation doc). Not 20–25% (vocabulary burden + drift-to-English risk); not 10% (under-represents a GEP-characteristic domain).

---

## 6. Observed-in-source vs our-recommended extension

**Observed in source materials:** analogies (frequent), anagram/word transformation, letter-number cipher, verbal deduction, sentence completion, vocabulary. Codes/letter patterns less systematically catalogsed in SG sources but present.

**Our recommended BrainActive extension:** add the UK-style **letter series, hidden word, move-a-letter** as explicit C-type skills; add a **verbal-deduction** strand distinct from sentence completion; keep analogy + classification as the semantic core; add **word manipulation** (currently *absent* from v0.4.1 — a real gap vs the framework's own 3.4).
