## 1. Singapore / MOE layer (2026 context)

### What changed (2024–2026)
- **Aug 2024** — MOE announced it would *strengthen* support for higher-ability learners: school-based programmes (e.g. E2K Math/Science) extended so **~10% of the cohort** can benefit (up from ~7%), identified from **Primary 4** (not only P3), with more holistic signals (teacher observation, student work), not just a single test.
- **Mar 2026** — MOE confirmed the **GEP in its current form will be discontinued from 2027**. From **2027** a *refreshed approach*: standardised **one-stage Primary 3 identification exercise** (replacing the old two-stage Screening+Selection), plus school-based identification, plus **weekly 2-hour advanced modules at 15 designated centres** in English/Math/Science. P3 students in **2026** are the first batch under the refreshed one-stage process (Aug 2026).
- **HAL (High Ability Learners)** programmes (E2K Math/Science, Maths Expert @ Work, Creative Writing, Wits & Words, etc.) run in **every primary school** and emphasise *inquiry, puzzles, games, mathematical explorations, critical & inventive thinking* — not syllabus acceleration.

### What this means for BrainActive
- The "GEP prep" search intent is shifting to **"high ability learner" / "higher-ability" / "E2K-style"** language. MOE itself now frames ability as *reasoning, potential, and interest*, identified over time — which fits a **daily practice app** better than a one-off test cram.
- We should **not** claim GEP prep or predict outcomes. We can legitimately say we build the *reasoning and thinking skills* that MOE's identification and E2K value.

**Valuable:** SG materials converge on *thinking skills + heuristics* (pattern, deduction, classification, spatial visualisation, working backwards, guess-&-check, before-after, drawing models). This is a distinct, defensible Singapore flavour we can own.
**Gaps:** Most SG materials are still maths-syllabus-bound or paper workbooks; **none is a game-like, daily, mixed-reasoning app** with deterministic visual generation and teaching explanations. That is our opening.

## 3. International reasoning layer

### CogAT (Cognitive Abilities Test, Riverside) — the gold-standard framework
Three batteries, each with 3 subtests (grades K–12):
- **Verbal:** Picture/Verbal Analogies, Sentence Completion, Verbal Classification.
- **Quantitative:** Number Analogies, Number Puzzles, Number Series.
- **Nonverbal (Figural):** Figure Matrices, Paper Folding, Figure Classification.
Grade 3+ is text-based; the design goal is *"reasoning skills students have developed to solve problems they have not been directly taught."* (riversideinsights.com CogAT brochure & test descriptions.)

### UK 11+ Non-Verbal & Verbal Reasoning
- **NVR types:** series/sequences, analogies, 2×2 & 3×3 matrices, odd-one-out, reflection, rotation, nets/cubes/3D, hidden/combining shapes, paper folding, codes. Reported mix: sequences ~20–25%, analogies & odd-one-out ~15–20% each, matrices/3D/reflection ~10–15%. (prep4all.co.uk; taylor-tuition.co.uk; blog.thinkacademy.uk; achievelearning.co.uk; twinkl.co.uk.)
- **VR types:** synonyms/antonyms, codes/letter substitution, anagrams, comprehension/inference, missing letters.

**Useful gaps SG materials under-cover:** systematic **figure matrices**, **paper folding**, **hidden/combining shapes**, and explicit **analogy/odd-one-out** training as standalone skills — all map cleanly to a deterministic visual generator.

## 4. Synthesis: what a P3 high-ability child should practise
Not "P3 maths + IQ + GEP questions." Rather: a balanced diet of **quantitative reasoning, logic, verbal reasoning, visual/spatial reasoning, abstract pattern reasoning, and problem-solving heuristics** — with the SG "thinking skills & heuristics" identity on top, delivered game-like and daily.
